HEADLINE: the gap is not an artifact of ridge. Three of the four surrogates overpromise
in 86 to 99.8 percent of cells.

THE RANDOM FOREST IS THE EXCEPTION AND THE THEORY PREDICTS IT. A forest output is an
average over trees, so it already approximates E[y | x]; Proposition 3.1 says a
conditional mean is unbiased on any selected subset. Its median gap is -0.015 and it
overpromises in only 16.7 percent of cells. This is the strongest single piece of
evidence for the misspecification framing over the selection-bias framing: the one
surrogate that is itself an average shows essentially no gap.

THE GAP DOES NOT TRACK ACCURACY. Gradient boosting has the LOWEST rank accuracy
(rho 0.258) and the LARGEST gap (+0.455); the random forest has middling accuracy
(0.501) and no gap. So the gap is set by the form of the reported score, not by how
well the model ranks, which is what Corollary 3.3 asserts.

Isotonic recalibration helps every surrogate: ridge 0.254 -> 0.111, rf 0.164 -> 0.103,
gbm 0.515 -> 0.186, knn 0.196 -> 0.160.
